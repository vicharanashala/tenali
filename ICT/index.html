So, What Are Chance and Probability?

Every day we face events where we aren't 100% sure what will happen — like whether it will rain today, who wins a cricket coin toss, or what number pops up on a Ludo die.

Chance is our everyday word for how likely something is to happen. Probability is how mathematics measures that chance using a number from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain)!

🌦️ Weather Forecast: "70% chance of rain" 🏏 Cricket Toss: "50-50 chance of Heads" 🎲 Ludo Game: "1 in 6 chance of rolling a 6" 🎟️ Lucky Draw: "1 winning ticket out of 100"
Section 2 • Core Concepts

Learn the Mathematics

Let's build your probability toolkit step by step — in simple English, with zero scary jargon.

🎯

1. What is Probability?

Probability is a mathematical measure of how likely an event is to happen. It turns words like "unlikely", "fifty-fifty", and "very likely" into exact numbers between 0 and 1.

Example: A coin has a probability of 1/2 (or 50%) of landing on Heads.
🧪

2. What is an Experiment?

An experiment (or trial) is any activity or action that produces a result we cannot predict with 100% certainty beforehand.

Example: Tossing a coin, rolling a die, or picking a ball from a bag without looking.
✨

3. What is an Outcome?

An outcome is a single possible result of an experiment.

Example: Getting Heads when you toss a coin, or rolling a 4 on a die.
📦

4. Total Possible Outcomes

Possible outcomes are ALL the different results that could happen in an experiment (also called the Sample Space).

Example: Rolling a die has 6 possible outcomes: {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.
⭐

5. Favourable Outcomes

Favourable outcomes are the specific results that you want or are looking for in an event.

Example: If you want an even number on a die, the favourable outcomes are {2, 4, 6} — that's 3 outcomes!
⚖️

6. Types of Events

  • Impossible Event (P = 0): Can NEVER happen (e.g., rolling a 7 on a standard die).
  • Certain Event (P = 1): Will ALWAYS happen (e.g., rolling a number from 1 to 6).
  • Equally Likely Events: Every outcome has the exact same chance (e.g., Heads and Tails on a fair coin).

📐 The Golden Formula of Probability
P(Event) =
Number of Favourable Outcomes Total Number of Possible Outcomes
Top Number (Numerator)

Count how many ways what you want can happen.

Bottom Number (Denominator)

Count all possible results that could happen (assuming each is equally likely).

📝 Step-by-Step Worked Examples (No Steps Skipped!)

Problem: A fair coin is tossed once. What is the probability of getting Heads?
Heads (H) ✓ Tails (T)
  1. Step 1 – List all possible outcomes: When you toss a coin, you can get Heads (H) or Tails (T). So, Total possible outcomes = 2.
  2. Step 2 – Count favourable outcomes: We want Heads. Only 1 outcome is Heads. So, Favourable outcomes = 1.
  3. Step 3 – Put values into the formula: P(Heads) = Favourable ÷ Total = 1 / 2.
  4. Step 4 – Convert to decimal & percentage: 1 ÷ 2 = 0.5, which equals 50%.
Problem: A fair 6-sided die is rolled once. What is the probability of getting an even number?
1 2 ✓ 3 4 ✓ 5 6 ✓
  1. Step 1 – List all possible outcomes: A standard die has faces {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. So, Total possible outcomes = 6.
  2. Step 2 – Count favourable outcomes: Even numbers in {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} are 2, 4, and 6. So, Favourable outcomes = 3.
  3. Step 3 – Apply the formula: P(Even) = 3 / 6.
  4. Step 4 – Simplify the fraction: Divide top and bottom by 3 → 3 ÷ 3 = 1 and 6 ÷ 3 = 2. So, P(Even) = 1 / 2 = 0.5 = 50%.
Problem: A bag contains 3 Red, 2 Blue, and 5 Green balls. One ball is picked at random without looking. What is the probability of picking a Red ball?
RRR BB GGGGG
  1. Step 1 – Find total number of balls: 3 Red + 2 Blue + 5 Green = 10 total possible outcomes.
  2. Step 2 – Count favourable outcomes: There are 3 Red balls.
  3. Step 3 – Put values into the formula: P(Red) = 3 / 10.
  4. Step 4 – Convert to decimal & percentage: 3 ÷ 10 = 0.3, which equals 30%.

Section 7 • Final Takeaways

What Did We Discover?

Mathematics cannot tell us the exact result of a single coin flip — but it CAN predict patterns and measure luck accurately!

01

Measures Likelihood

Probability tells us how likely an event is to happen using clear numbers instead of guesses.

02

Impossible Event = 0

If an event can never happen (like rolling an 8 on a regular die), its probability is 0.

03

Certain Event = 1

If an event is guaranteed to happen (like the sun rising tomorrow), its probability is 1.

04

Always Between 0 and 1

For every event, 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1. Probability can never be negative or greater than 1 (100%).

05

The Formula

When outcomes are equally likely: P(E) = Favourable Outcomes ÷ Total Possible Outcomes.

06

Experiments Vary, Patterns Stabilize

Experimental probability changes when repeated, but with many trials it gets closer to theoretical probability.
