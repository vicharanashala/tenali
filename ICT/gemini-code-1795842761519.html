What is the Doppler Effect?

The Doppler Effect is the apparent change in the frequency (or pitch) of a wave when there is relative motion between the source of the wave and the observer.

Everyday Example: When an ambulance approaches you, its siren sounds higher-pitched because the sound waves get compressed. As it moves away, the waves stretch out, so the siren sounds lower-pitched.

The Master Formula (Class 11/12)

f' = f × v ± vov ∓ vs
  • f' = Apparent frequency heard by observer (Hz)
  • f = Actual frequency emitted by source (Hz)
  • v = Speed of sound in the medium (~340 m/s in air)
  • vo = Speed of the observer (m/s)
  • vs = Speed of the source (m/s)

Easy Sign Convention Trick

Never memorize 8 separate formulas! Just remember one rule:

Moving TOWARDS each other → Pitch INCREASES (f' > f)

Use +vo in the numerator and −vs in the denominator.

Moving AWAY from each other → Pitch DECREASES (f' < f)

Use −vo in the numerator and +vs in the denominator.

Important Exam Cases at a Glance

Case Condition Formula Effect on Pitch
1. Source approaches stationary observer vo = 0, source toward O f' = f × v / (v − vs) Increases (f' > f)
2. Source recedes from stationary observer vo = 0, source away from O f' = f × v / (v + vs) Decreases (f' < f)
3. Observer approaches stationary source vs = 0, observer toward S f' = f × (v + vo) / v Increases (f' > f)
4. Observer recedes from stationary source vs = 0, observer away from S f' = f × (v − vo) / v Decreases (f' < f)
