🌍 Changes Around Us

Learn how things change – then test yourself with puzzles and games!

What is a change?

👉 A change is when something becomes different from before.

Examples: ice melts 🧊, milk turns into curd 🥛, iron rusts 🔩, a seed grows into a plant 🌱.

⏱️ Slow & Fast

Slow: growing of a plant, rusting of iron, ageing.

Fast: bursting a balloon, burning a match, lightning.

↩️ Reversible & Irreversible

Reversible changes can be undone: melting and freezing ice, folding paper.

Irreversible changes cannot be undone: baking a cake, burning wood, cooking an egg.

🔁 Periodic & Non-periodic

Periodic changes repeat after a fixed time: day and night, seasons, the Moon's phases.

Non-periodic changes do not repeat in a fixed way: earthquakes, rain, a falling tree.

Physical vs Chemical changes

🧊 Physical change

  • Only shape, size, colour or state changes.
  • No new substance is formed.
  • Usually reversible.

Examples: melting ice, boiling water, cutting paper, dissolving sugar.

🔥 Chemical change

  • One or more new substances are formed.
  • Usually cannot be reversed easily.
  • Often involves heat, light, gas, smell or a colour change.

Examples: burning paper, rusting, curdling of milk, cooking food.

🕵️ 4 clues of a chemical change

  1. A new substance appears (like ash or rust).
  2. A colour change you cannot undo.
  3. A gas is given out (bubbles or a new smell).
  4. Heat, light or sound is produced or absorbed.

🏠 Try it at home (with an adult)

Sugar in water

Stir a spoon of sugar in warm water. It seems to vanish. Let the water evaporate in a plate – the sugar comes back! Physical change.

Rusting nails

Keep one iron nail in a dry jar and another in a jar of moist air for a week. Which one rusts? Chemical change.

Ice race

Put an ice cube in sunlight and another in shade. Time how long each takes to melt. Physical change.

💡 Fun fact: Freshly cut apples turn brown because oxygen in the air reacts with substances in the fruit – a chemical change. A drop of lemon juice slows it down!
